It can be frustrating when payment processors place a hold on or freeze a merchant's hard-earned funds. Frozen funds can prevent business owners from accessing their revenue and make it difficult to cover essential expenses, including employee wages, vendor payments, and utility bills. In this article, we'll take a look at seven common reasons payment processors hold money from merchants.

## **1. New Account Reviews**

Many new merchants or merchants without a payment processing history may be under stricter monitoring from their payment service processor or may have "rolling reserve" requirements that they must comply with.

## **2. Changes in Sales Activity**

A sudden increase in sales volume or unusually large transactions can cause a payment processor to flag your account for review. For example, if a retail shop typically processes transactions around $65, a one-time $4,000 purchase could appear unusual and trigger additional scrutiny.
