It can be frustrating when payment processors place a hold on or freeze a merchant's hard-earned funds. Frozen funds can prevent business owners from accessing their revenue and make it difficult to cover essential expenses, including employee wages, vendor payments, and utility bills. In this article, we'll take a look at seven common reasons payment processors hold money from merchants.

## **1. New Account Reviews**

Many new merchants or merchants without a payment processing history may be under stricter monitoring from their payment service processor or may have "rolling reserve" requirements that they must comply with.

## **2. Changes in Sales Activity**

A sudden increase in sales volume or unusually large transactions can cause a payment processor to flag your account for review. For example, if a retail shop typically processes transactions around $65, a one-time $4,000 purchase could appear unusual and trigger additional scrutiny.

## **5. Minimal Upfront Underwriting**

Payment service providers that rely on aggregated merchant accounts often prioritize onboarding your business quickly, with underwriting performed afterward rather than before approval. This reversed process can result in incomplete or surface-level risk assessments.

As a consequence, even fully legitimate businesses may be flagged, restricted, or shut down due to unclear or misunderstood business models, leaving merchants with little recourse or explanation.

## **6. Aggregated Merchant Accounts**

Some payment service providers, such as Stripe or PayPal, operate using a single master merchant account through which they route and process transactions. In this model, your business is pooled together with other merchants, meaning you share risk and liability.

As a result, statement descriptors often display the aggregator's name first, which can create customer confusion and increase the likelihood of chargebacks. Additionally, you typically do not have a direct relationship with the acquiring bank, limiting your ability to negotiate terms or effectively defend your business model if issues like frozen funds do arise.
